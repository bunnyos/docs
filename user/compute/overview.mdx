Every answer bunny gives is generated by a real machine that somebody contributed to **bunnyCompute**, the network behind bunnyOS. Yours can be one of them. You run a small worker program on your own computer. It picks up requests from the network and answers them right on your machine. You earn [Carrots](/user/carrots/explained) for the work.

## The deal

The economics are simple. Every job your worker serves earns you Carrots, paid out of what the request cost the person asking. About 80% of each request goes straight to the machine that did the work. The rest keeps the network running.

If a job fails, the person who asked gets a full refund and the worker is paid nothing for it. There is no penalty beyond the missed earning, so an occasional hiccup costs you nothing but the job.

Your earnings land in your Carrots history automatically as **Worker earning** entries, one per job. There is nothing to claim and no button to press. See [Your Carrots and usage](/user/carrots/your-carrots) to watch them arrive.

<Note>
Carrots you earn behave exactly like Carrots you were granted or staked for. You can spend them chatting with bunny, or through the [Inference API](/user/developer/inference-api).
</Note>

## What your machine needs

You need a computer with a real GPU or Apple Silicon, left switched on and connected while you want to earn. The network is judged on how quickly people get answers, so every worker is measured for speed when it joins. Machines that are too slow are turned away. The full list of requirements and the setup steps live on [Run a worker](/user/compute/native-worker).

## Watching the network

Two screens in [the Mini App](/user/getting-started/mini-app) show you how things are going.

- **Network Stats** shows how many workers are online for each model right now, plus all-time totals for requests served and tokens generated. These are network-wide numbers across everybody contributing, not your own.
- **Supply Compute** shows your own workers, each with a live status of **Offline**, **Connecting**, **Idle**, or **Working**, and when it was last seen.

<Tip>
**Network Stats** also shows how many requests are queued for each model. A queue means requests are waiting for a worker to pick them up.
</Tip>
